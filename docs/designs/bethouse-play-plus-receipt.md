## The one-paragraph version

BetHouse has users: the author and his friends, using it to pick legs for parlays. The
goal is to prove a winning percentage and monetize it.

The obstacle is not demand, not model quality, and not infrastructure. It is the vig.
DraftKings' measured hold on the 1+ H/R/RBI market is **6.96%**, and at that price a
three-leg slip of perfectly calibrated legs returns **-15.4%**. A model that is exactly
right still loses. So the sellable claim can never be a hit rate.

But the same arithmetic contains the answer. Parlay EV is `m^n x correlation`, where `m`
is the per-leg edge over the book's price. That exponent cuts both ways: with no edge it
destroys you, and with real edge it is the most efficient way to express one. At a 5%
per-leg edge a three-leg slip returns **+22%**. **The parlay instinct is correct; it is
just leverage, and leverage on zero edge is a loss.**

So the whole business reduces to one measurable question: does BetHouse's number beat
the book's number, per leg, consistently? That is closing line value, it started
collecting on 23 August, and it is the only thing worth proving.

## The constraint that decides everything

Measured, not assumed. From `odds-data.js` on origin at 2026-08-24T21:36Z, 244 live
markets: **median hold 6.96% on hrr** (n=180, range 6.76-7.21%) and 6.93% on total bases
(n=64).

A perfectly calibrated 66.6% leg priced into a 6.96% hold gives a per-leg multiplier of
`m = 0.9304`. Parlay EV is `m^n x correlation` (1.05x for cross-game slips, per the
README's measured 1.03-1.08x):

| legs | hits | expected return, no edge |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 66.6% | -7.0% |
| 2 | 46.6% | -9.1% |
| 3 | 31.0% | **-15.4%** |
| 4 | 20.7% | -21.3% |

**The model is right and the bet still loses.** A three-leg slip cashes 31% of the time,
which sounds like a good winning percentage, and bleeds 15.4% of stake.
`index.html:1694` already says this on the board: at standard juice most of these are -EV.

**The same exponent is the opportunity.** `m^n` is leverage. Applied to zero edge it is
ruinous; applied to real edge it is the most efficient expression available:

| per-leg edge | 1-leg | 2-leg | 3-leg | 4-leg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| m = 1.03 | +3% | +11% | +15% | +18% |
| m = 1.05 | +5% | +16% | +22% | +28% |
| m = 1.08 | +8% | +22% | +32% | +43% |

**The parlay instinct is right. It is leverage, and it is only ruinous on zero edge.**

The break-even target is concrete and checkable: a three-leg slip needs `m >= 0.9839`
against today's `m = 0.9304`, which is a **5.7% relative improvement over the book's
price, per leg.** Not a vague aspiration. A number that can be measured every day.

Consequence for the business: "our picks hit X%" cannot be the claim. "Our number beats
the closing number by enough to clear 5.7% per leg" is the claim, and it is measurable.

## What is actually provable, and therefore sellable

**Closing line value.** Whether the number taken was better than the number the market
closed at, measured in no-vig probability points.

It cannot be faked by picking favourites, cannot be flattered by a lucky week, and is the
measure books themselves use to decide who to limit. "Our picks beat the closing line by
N points across M bets, all published before first pitch" is a claim almost nobody in
this industry makes, and it is the one that survives an audit.

The plumbing exists: `legClv` and `clvOf` at `bets.js:269-281`, `close-odds.mjs` wired
into `refresh-odds.yml:58`.

**And the data has started.** `odds-close/2026-08-23.json` holds 418 frozen closing
prices and `odds-close/2026-08-24.json` holds 248. The feed is healthy (244 live markets,
refreshing hourly) and the workflow at `refresh-odds.yml:58` is running. The clock started
on 23 August without anyone deciding to start it.

What is missing is the read side: nothing aggregates those frozen prices into a running
CLV figure. `clvOf` only sees bets a user manually tapped, and there are three of those.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Start both clocks today (chosen)

Two collectors, both modelled on the three rules already in `track-core.mjs`: first
prediction wins, pregame only by the clock, nothing backfilled.

**A1. The parlay forward record.** `track.mjs` records single legs and has never recorded
a parlay; the number the business would sell has never been measured. Each morning,
before first pitch, snapshot what `S.suggestParlay` (`score.js:961`) returned, including
each leg's calibrated probability and the combined number from `S.combineLegs`
(`score.js:873`). Grade it after. Thirty days produces an honest forward parlay record
for the current model.

**A2. Read the CLV that is already being collected.** Two days of frozen closing prices
exist. Nothing reads them in aggregate: `clvOf` (`bets.js:277`) only evaluates bets a
user tapped by hand. The missing piece is a job that joins every recorded prediction to
its frozen closing price and reports mean CLV per prop. That is the number the business
is actually built on, and the raw material is already on disk.

Effort S-M. The collection is done; this is the read side.

**Why this is first:** every other option gets better with time and this one gets
strictly worse. A record started today is thirty days old in thirty days. This is the
only item on the list with that property, and two days of it are already banked.

### Approach B: Ship the consumer surface first

The Slip: one page, today's play, plain-English reasons, record underneath. This was the
previous draft's recommendation, chosen when demand was believed to be zero.

Rejected: demand is no longer the unknown. Building a surface to acquire users the author
already has, while the proof those users would eventually be sold does not exist, is
solving yesterday's bottleneck.

Worth building once there is something to put on the bottom half of the page.

### Approach C: Design the paid product

Pricing, tiers, payments, terms. Premature. There is nothing to sell yet, and the
arithmetic above means the obvious claim would be false. Revisit when A has produced
thirty days of record.

## Recommended Approach

**A**, on the grounds that it is the only work whose value decays if delayed.

Sequence: start collecting today, build the surface (B) while the record accumulates,
design the product (C) only if the record supports a claim. If after thirty days the
parlay record shows what the arithmetic predicts, that is not a failure of the project.
It is the project working, and it means the honest thing to sell is the CLV number rather
than a win rate.

## Open Questions

- **Are the friends up or down?** Unknown, and it is the most important unknown in this
  document. If the people closest to the product are losing money, a winning-percentage
  claim dies on contact with them first.
- **How many friends, how often?** No instrumentation exists. A `bethouse.visits.v1`
  localStorage counter would answer this at zero cost and within the constraints.
- **Did the recalibration over-correct on hrr?** The first post-recalibration day
  (2026-08-23, n=271) shows hrr predicted 66.6% against actual 70.8%, a **-4.30pp** cold
  bias, and tb2 -3.58pp. One day at n=271 has a standard error near 2.9pp, so -4.3pp is
  about 1.5 standard deviations, which is noise and must not be acted on. But the
  direction is the one `tasks/lessons.md` warns about: the shrink was deliberately
  shipped at the timid end, and over-correction costs money. Watch it; do not touch it
  until n is in the thousands.
- **Does the model clear 5.7% per leg?** The single question the business rests on.
  Answerable from data already on disk.
- **What decides the existing record's fate?** Label it as spanning a model change,
  restrict it to post-recalibration days, or argue it still counts. Unresolved by
  choice; see Known Concerns.
- **What is actually being sold?** Picks, or the record itself, or access to the tool.
  These have different legal and product shapes.

## Success Criteria

1. **A parlay is snapshotted before first pitch and graded after.** Binary, and it is
   the one collector that does not yet exist.
2. **A mean CLV figure exists across all recorded predictions**, not just the three
   hand-tapped bets. The frozen prices are already there.
3. Thirty consecutive days of both, unbroken. Gaps are permanent.
4. At the thirty-day mark: a parlay win rate with its `n`, and a mean CLV with its `n`,
   both for the post-22-August model only, compared against the 5.7% per-leg bar.
5. Nothing regresses: `bash scripts/local-check.sh` green, all tests passing with none
   removed or weakened.

Explicitly not success criteria: a good-looking win rate over one week, or any claim made
before criterion 4 exists.

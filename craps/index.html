How to Play Craps

🎯 Strategy & Betting Principles

The golden rule: The Odds bet is the only wager in the casino with a 0% house edge. Every winning strategy is built on making a small flat bet and backing it with as much Odds as you can.

System 1 — Pass Line + Full Odds (the foundation)

Bet the Pass Line. When a point is set, back it with the maximum Odds you can afford (this table allows 3-4-5×). You blend the 1.41% flat edge down toward the 0% odds edge — with full 3-4-5× odds your combined edge drops to about 0.37%. This is the lowest-risk way to play. Turn on Auto-take odds to do it automatically.

System 2 — The 3-Point Molly

The gold-standard grind strategy. Always keep bets working on up to three numbers:

  1. Bet the Pass Line; take odds when the point is set.
  2. Make a Come bet; take odds when it travels to a number.
  3. Make a second Come bet; take odds again.

Now you win on almost every box number the shooter hits, and only a 7 hurts you. Every bet in the Molly carries the table's lowest edge. Stop at three numbers so a seven-out doesn't wipe out too much.

System 3 — Place the 6 & 8

The 6 and 8 are the most common numbers after 7. Placing both (in multiples of $6) at only a 1.52% edge is a solid, low-variance way to get action without touching the line. Take them down whenever you feel the shooter cooling.

System 4 — Dark Side (Don't Pass + Lay Odds)

Betting Don't Pass then laying odds has an even lower edge (1.36% → ~0.27% with full odds). After the come-out you're statistically favored on every roll (a 7 helps you). The trade-off: you lay more to win less, and you'll be quietly rooting against the whole table.

Bankroll management: Bring at least 20–30× your base bet. Decide a win goal and a loss limit before you start, and walk when you hit either. Never chase losses by jumping to high-edge prop bets — that's how bankrolls die. Press (increase) bets with the house's money, not your buy-in.

What to avoid

The center of the table (props, hardways, any seven, horn) is where the house makes its money — edges of 9% to 17%. They're thrilling and occasionally hand you a big pop, but over time they bleed you fast. Treat them as entertainment with a tiny fraction of your bankroll, never as a strategy.
